# Evolution by publication cohort

Publication year is not a project's start date, a deployment date or a partnership start date. Counts describe the selected set and are not annualized for 2026. See the HTML report's [topic evolution](../output/tencent_networking_report.html#topics) and [source chart data](../output/analysis_year_topics.csv).

## 2021–2022: cloud access and infrastructure visibility

DSR separates cloud-router functions; Looking Glass discovery broadens external measurement coverage. OpTel adds one-second optical telemetry; Elixir addresses bursty hardware/software flow-table placement. These papers establish different capabilities, not a proven single integrated architecture.

## 2023–2024: resilience, fine granularity and experience

TENSOR targets BGP continuity; FlexWAN exploits optical flexibility; AFR exposes decoder-induced latency. In 2024, MegaTE extends WAN TE to virtual instances, while AUGUR, Hairpin and Pudica explore multipath, recovery and congestion behavior in interactive streaming. Turbo connects RDMA communication to completed data-processing jobs. Boomerang is a distinct privacy research connection, not an assumed deployed Tencent messaging feature.

## 2025: observability and AI-scale infrastructure

Holmes studies silent training irregularities; Astral co-designs networking, facilities and monitoring. P-ECMP exposes constrained precise traffic control, Fornax changes session management, and PreTE makes failure probabilities dynamic. The five-paper cohort is small; two explicitly AI-workload papers are 40% of it, not evidence of an across-company investment ratio.

## 2026: extend the stack, revisit new bottlenecks

The 14-paper dated cohort includes MoE communication (SwiftEP), disaggregated AI-cloud DPUs (DistDPU), bare-metal AI networking (Pegasus), session memory (Dorado) and new-flow setup (XFir). Janus, Canopy and Harp target peering/security/failure handling. MirSwitch, MirrorNet and CubeTrace make infrastructure testable and diagnosable. BLADE and LADR revisit wireless and loss sources. Hestia remains the explicit cloud-resource boundary case.

Only three of these 14 papers explicitly target AI workloads. This is not a monotonic rise in annual AI share; it is a recent addition to a broader portfolio. NUS's five appearances in 2026 partly coincide with a recurring coauthor's changed/combined listed affiliations; this does not prove five newly initiated institutional projects.

## Next hypotheses—not a known roadmap

Investigate joint AI placement/collectives/isolation; flexible offload plus verification; application-to-MAC QoE control; and risk-aware WAN control coupled with replayable tests. Each hypothesis needs later papers, artifacts or public technical evidence before being treated as Tencent strategy.
